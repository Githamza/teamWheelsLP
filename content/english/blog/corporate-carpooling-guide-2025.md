## 1. What Is Ridesharing — and What Is Carpooling? {#definitions}

> **Definition** — Ridesharing is any arrangement where two or more people share a vehicle journey. It covers two distinct models: **ride-hailing** (booking a professional driver through an app like Uber or Bolt — you pay per trip) and **carpooling** (sharing a privately-owned vehicle with peers, splitting the actual cost of fuel and tolls, with no driver fee). For businesses, these two models have radically different cost profiles.

The confusion between ridesharing and carpooling matters enormously for company finance teams. When employees expense Uber rides for their daily commute, you're paying 3 to 5 times the actual trip cost — every driver surcharge, platform fee, and surge multiplier comes out of your budget. Organised carpooling eliminates all of this: participants share only the genuine cost of the journey.

> **What Is Carpooling — Clear Definition**
>
> Carpooling is the shared use of a privately-owned vehicle between two or more people travelling the same route — typically a recurring commute. There is no professional driver and no platform commission. Costs (fuel, tolls, parking) are divided between participants. For companies, carpooling is the most cost-effective, sustainable, and scalable form of employee mobility — and it qualifies for meaningful tax benefits in the US, UK, and Australia.

## 2. Ridesharing Companies in 2025 — and What They Miss for Business {#landscape}

The ridesharing industry is dominated by a handful of global platforms. Understanding where each one fits — and where they fall short for enterprise needs — is essential before choosing a mobility solution.

### The ride-hailing giants

**Uber** is the world's largest ridesharing company by revenue, operating in 70+ countries. **Bolt** leads across Europe and Africa. Both are ride-hailing services: gig-economy drivers pick up individual passengers on demand. They are exceptional for occasional, ad-hoc travel. They are expensive, uncontrollable, and unsustainable for daily commuting at scale.

### The long-distance carpooling platform

**BlaBlaCar** pioneered peer-to-peer carpooling for journeys over 100 miles — intercity travel, holiday trips, airport runs. It was not designed for 8am–6pm corporate commutes, does not integrate with enterprise tools, and has no HR or ESG reporting layer.

## 3. Country-Specific Tax & Regulatory Benefits {#markets}

One of the most compelling arguments for corporate carpooling is that each market offers its own financial incentives — making the ROI case straightforward to build for any CFO.

### 🇺🇸 United States

- **IRS Section 132(f):** Employees can exclude up to **$315/month (2025)** in employer-provided commuter benefits from taxable income — see our [US Scope 3 compliance guide](/blog/scope-3-employee-commuting-corporate-carpooling-usa-compliance-guide/)
- Pre-tax payroll deduction reduces employer **FICA tax** burden
- **Vanpool alternatives:** Organised carpooling qualifies where traditional vanpools are impractical
- **LEED / WELL certification:** Carpool programmes earn sustainability credits
- **State incentives:** California, Washington, and New York offer additional employer-side credits for certified commuter programmes

### 🇬🇧 United Kingdom

- **Clean Air Zones (CAZ):** Carpooling reduces vehicles entering Birmingham, Bath, Bradford, and Bristol CAZs — cutting daily charges of £8–£12 per vehicle
- **NIC savings:** Employer-facilitated car-sharing reduces National Insurance contributions on company car benefits
- **EV carpooling:** HMRC's Advisory Electricity Rate (AER) makes shared EV commuting particularly cost-efficient
- **SECR reporting:** Corporate carpooling programmes generate auditable Scope 3 commute data for Streamlined Energy and Carbon Reporting — see our [UK employee carpooling complete guide](/blog/uk-employee-carpooling-complete-guide/)
- **Net Zero Transport Plan:** Direct alignment with government-mandated sustainability targets

### 🇦🇺 Australia

- **FBT exemptions:** Properly structured carpooling arrangements can qualify for the work-related travel exemption, reducing Fringe Benefits Tax liability
- **9-passenger threshold:** Organised carpooling helps companies move toward the FBT-exempt category for larger vehicles
- **State sustainability grants:** VIC, NSW, and QLD offer SME mobility grants for certified commuter programmes
- **ASX ESG reporting:** Scope 3 commute emissions reduction is increasingly disclosed in ASX 300 sustainability reports
