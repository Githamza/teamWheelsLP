# Sustainable Mobility Incentives in 2026: The Employer's Commuter Benefits Playbook (UK & US)

*A practical, side-by-side guide to the reimbursement thresholds, allowance caps and payment mechanisms HR, benefits and finance leaders need to run a compliant, high-ROI commuter programme on both sides of the Atlantic.*

Commuter benefits have quietly become one of the most cost-effective levers in the total-rewards toolkit. They cut employees' out-of-pocket travel costs, shrink an employer's Scope 3 commuting footprint, and — when structured correctly — reduce payroll tax for both sides. Yet the rules differ sharply between the United States and the United Kingdom, and several 2026 changes have reshaped what actually qualifies.

This playbook lays out the current thresholds, the eligible transport modes, and the administrative plumbing (pre-tax payroll, salary sacrifice, mobility cards, expense reimbursement) so you can build a programme that survives an audit and a CFO review. Throughout, we position **carpooling and vanpooling as the highest-ROI eligible mode** — they qualify for the same tax treatment as transit but need no vehicles to lease or charge points to install.

> A note on figures: statutory caps are indexed and change at fiscal events. Every monthly limit and per-mile rate below carries a "verify current figure" flag and a link to the issuing authority. Confirm the live number before you communicate it to employees.

## Why Sustainable Mobility Incentives Matter More in 2026

Three forces have pushed commuter incentives up the boardroom agenda:

- **Scope 3 disclosure is going mainstream.** The EU CSRD, the UK's climate-related disclosure regime and California's SB 253 all push employers to quantify employee-commuting emissions. Mode-shift incentives are one of the few Category 7 levers an employer directly controls.
- **Cost-of-living pressure on employees.** Fuel and parking remain expensive. A pre-tax or salary-sacrifice benefit that saves an employee 28–47% on commuting costs is a genuine retention tool.
- **Municipal mandates are spreading.** A growing list of US cities now legally requires employers above a headcount threshold to offer pre-tax commuter benefits.

The good news: most of the tax machinery already exists. The job is to configure it correctly and pick the modes with the best return.

## United States: Section 132(f) Qualified Transportation Fringe Benefits

The backbone of US commuter benefits is **IRC Section 132(f)**, which lets employees pay for certain commuting costs with pre-tax income — reducing both income tax and FICA — via a payroll deduction or an employer-funded subsidy. The IRS sets the monthly excludable amounts each year through a Revenue Procedure ([IRS Publication 15-B](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b)).

### The 2026 Monthly Pre-Tax Caps

| Qualified transportation fringe (Section 132(f)) | 2025 monthly cap | 2026 monthly cap | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transit passes | $325 | **$340** | Verify current figure — [IRS Pub 15-B](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b) (Rev. Proc. 2025-32) |
| Commuter highway vehicle / **vanpool** | $325 | **$340** | Shares the same combined monthly limit as transit |
| Qualified parking | $325 | **$340** | Separate limit — can be used **in addition** to transit/vanpool |
| Qualified bicycle commuting reimbursement | Taxable | **Repealed** | Permanently ended for tax years after 31 Dec 2025 (OBBBA, P.L. 119-21) |

Two points HR and payroll teams frequently get wrong:

- **Transit and vanpool share one limit; parking is separate.** An employee can shelter up to $340/month for transit-or-vanpool *and* another $340/month for parking in the same month — but cannot exceed either cap individually.
- **The bicycle commuting reimbursement is gone.** The Tax Cuts and Jobs Act suspended the bicycle exclusion for 2018–2025, and it was scheduled to return in 2026. Instead, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act made the removal **permanent**. From 2026, any employer bike reimbursement is taxable wages to the employee. (E-bikes and bikeshare can still be delivered as an ordinary taxable perk, just without the exclusion.)

### The Employer Deduction Catch

Under TCJA changes to IRC Section 274, employers generally **cannot deduct** the cost of qualified transportation fringe benefits (transit and parking) provided to employees. In practice this means:

- **Pre-tax employee-funded deductions** still work well — the employer's FICA savings (7.65% of the sheltered amount) usually more than offset the compliance cost.
- **Employer-paid subsidies** deliver a strong benefit to employees but are not tax-deductible for the business, so model them as a net cost.

### City and State Commuter-Benefit Mandates

Several jurisdictions now require employers above a size threshold to offer pre-tax commuter benefits. Non-compliance carries escalating penalties.

| Jurisdiction | Employer threshold | In force since | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| New York City | 20+ full-time employees | 2016 | Must offer proactively; benefit level tracks the federal cap ($340 in 2026). [NYC Commuter Benefits Law](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/about/commuter-benefits-law.page) |
| Washington, DC | 20+ employees | 2010 | Pre-tax transit or employer-paid transit/vanpool |
| San Francisco Bay Area | 50+ (regional) | 2014 | SF city rules apply a lower threshold on top |
| New Jersey (statewide) | 20+ employees | 2019 | Pre-tax transit benefit required |
| Chicago, Philadelphia, Seattle | Varies (typically 20+) | Various | Confirm the local ordinance for exact rules |

If you operate in more than one of these markets, standardise on a single commuter-benefits administrator so the highest common denominator is met everywhere.

## United Kingdom: Salary Sacrifice, AMAP and EV Incentives

The UK has no single "commuter benefit account." Instead, employers assemble a portfolio of statutory reliefs — salary sacrifice schemes, tax-free mileage payments and benefit-in-kind exemptions.

### Cycle to Work: No Cap Since 2019

The [Cycle to Work scheme](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cycle-to-work-scheme-implementation-guidance) is a salary-sacrifice arrangement: the employee gives up gross pay in exchange for a bike and safety equipment, avoiding income tax and National Insurance on the sacrificed amount.

- **The £1,000 cap was removed in 2019.** High-value e-bikes and cargo bikes are firmly in scope; the certification an FCA-regulated provider needs above £1,000 is a paperwork step, not a barrier. See guidance from the [Energy Saving Trust](https://energysavingtrust.org.uk/).
- **Cycles are exempt from OpRA rules**, so the full salary-sacrifice advantage survives.
- **Savings by band:** basic-rate employees save ~28% (20% income tax + 8% NI), higher-rate ~42%, additional-rate ~47%.
- **Employers save** employer National Insurance (15% from April 2025) on the sacrificed salary.

### Mileage and Passenger Payments (AMAP)

When an employee uses their **own** car for business travel (not the ordinary commute), the employer can reimburse tax-free up to the [Approved Mileage Allowance Payment](https://www.gov.uk/expenses-and-benefits-business-travel-mileage/rules-for-tax) rates.

| AMAP element (2026-27) | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Car/van — first 10,000 business miles | **55p/mile** | Raised from 45p — first increase since 2011-12. Verify on [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/expenses-and-benefits-business-travel-mileage/rules-for-tax) |
| Car/van — miles above 10,000 | **25p/mile** | Unchanged |
| Motorcycles | 24p/mile | Unchanged |
| Bicycles | 20p/mile | Unchanged |
| **Passenger payment** (per colleague carried) | **5p/mile** | Tax-free top-up for carrying a co-worker on the same business trip |

The **5p/mile passenger rate** is the UK's direct carpooling incentive: a driver taking a colleague on a business journey can be paid an extra tax-free 5p per mile per passenger. It is modest per trip but a clean, HMRC-sanctioned way to reward shared travel. (Note: if the employer does not pay it, the passenger amount cannot be reclaimed through Mileage Allowance Relief — only the driver's own AMAP shortfall can.)

### EV Charging, Salary-Sacrifice Cars and Season-Ticket Loans

- **Workplace EV charging is a tax-free benefit.** Electricity provided by an employer to charge an employee's own EV **at or near the workplace**, made available to all staff, is exempt from benefit-in-kind tax.
- **EV salary sacrifice remains highly efficient.** The benefit-in-kind rate on fully electric company cars is 4% for 2026-27, keeping the effective cost of an EV lease through salary sacrifice well below a personal lease. Employees typically save 20–50% depending on tax band.
- **Interest-free season-ticket loans** let employers lend up to £10,000 for an annual rail or bus pass without triggering a taxable beneficial-loan charge, spreading the cost over the year.
- **Workplace Charging Scheme (OZEV):** from 1 April 2026 the grant rose to £500 per socket, up to a maximum of £20,000 per applicant — useful if you are also enabling EV carpools.

## Eligible Transport Modes at a Glance

Both regimes reward mode-shift, but through different instruments. Use this as a planning matrix:

- **Carpooling / vanpool** — US: pre-tax under Section 132(f) (vanpool shares the $340 transit limit); UK: 5p/mile tax-free passenger payment for business trips. **Highest ROI: no fleet, no infrastructure.**
- **Public transit** — US: pre-tax passes up to $340/month; UK: interest-free season-ticket loans.
- **Bicycles and e-bikes** — US: exclusion repealed (taxable perk only); UK: uncapped Cycle to Work salary sacrifice.
- **E-scooters and low-emission shared mobility** — US: not a qualified fringe (taxable); UK: no dedicated relief, sometimes bundled into a wider mobility budget.
- **Electric vehicles** — US: purchase incentives sit outside commuter fringes; UK: 4% BIK salary sacrifice + tax-free workplace charging.
- **Qualified parking** — US: separate $340/month pre-tax; UK: no direct equivalent.

## Administrative and Payment Mechanisms

The tax benefit is only as good as the delivery mechanism. The main options:

- **Pre-tax payroll deduction (US).** The employee elects an amount, payroll withholds it before tax, and it funds a transit or vanpool pass. Lowest cost, strong FICA savings.
- **Commuter benefit cards / FSA-style transit accounts (US).** A third-party administrator issues a restricted debit card usable only for eligible transit and parking — the compliance-friendly default for multi-city employers.
- **Expense reimbursement.** The employee pays and claims back against receipts. Simple, but reimbursements outside a qualified account may be taxable (as with bike reimbursements from 2026).
- **Salary sacrifice (UK).** The employee agrees to a lower gross salary in exchange for a benefit (bike, EV, pension). Both parties save NI. Requires a formal variation to the employment contract.
- **Mobility vouchers / mobility budgets.** A flexible allowance the employee spends across approved modes. Common in continental Europe; increasingly bundled into UK and US flexible-benefit platforms.

For carpooling and vanpooling specifically, the administrative burden is usually **matching, tracking and reimbursement** rather than tax mechanics — which is exactly where a dedicated platform pays for itself.

## Where Carpooling Delivers the Best Return

Across both regimes, carpooling and vanpooling stand out because they:

- **Qualify for favourable tax treatment** (US vanpool pre-tax; UK 5p passenger payment) without the employer buying or leasing anything.
- **Need no physical infrastructure** — unlike EV charging points or cycle storage.
- **Cut Scope 3 commuting emissions fastest per pound or dollar spent** — a two-person carpool roughly halves per-capita commuting CO₂.
- **Free up parking**, converting a capped, non-deductible parking cost (US) into real-estate savings.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What are the 2026 pre-tax commuter benefit limits in the US?**
For 2026, employees can set aside up to $340 per month pre-tax for transit passes and vanpool commuting, plus a separate $340 per month for qualified parking, under IRC Section 132(f). Both limits rose from $325 in 2025. Always confirm the current cap in the latest IRS Revenue Procedure, as the amount is adjusted annually for inflation.

**Is carpooling or vanpooling an eligible commuter benefit?**
Yes. Commuting in a qualified commuter highway vehicle — commonly a vanpool seating at least six adults besides the driver — is one of the three benefits employees can pay for pre-tax under US Section 132(f), sharing the $340 transit limit. In the UK, drivers carrying colleagues can claim a tax-free 5p/mile passenger payment. Carpooling is typically the highest-ROI eligible mode because it needs no fleet or infrastructure.

**Is there still a £1,000 cap on the UK Cycle to Work scheme?**
No. The £1,000 limit was removed in 2019, so employers can now provide bikes, e-bikes and cargo bikes of any value through salary sacrifice. Cycles and safety equipment are exempt from the Optional Remuneration Arrangement rules, so the full tax and National Insurance advantage applies. Employees typically save 28–47% depending on their tax band, and employers save employer National Insurance.

**What are the 2026-27 UK mileage and passenger reimbursement rates?**
From 6 April 2026 the Approved Mileage Allowance Payment rate for cars and vans is 55p per mile for the first 10,000 business miles and 25p per mile thereafter, up from 45p following the first increase since 2011-12. The tax-free passenger rate for carrying a colleague on the same business trip stays at 5p per mile. Confirm the current figures on GOV.UK, as rates can change at fiscal events.

**Can employees combine multiple sustainable mobility incentives?**
Generally yes, provided each benefit stays within its own statutory limit. A US employee can use pre-tax transit dollars and a separate pre-tax parking allowance in the same month, but cannot exceed either monthly cap. A UK employee can hold a Cycle to Work agreement, an EV salary-sacrifice car and claim mileage or passenger payments simultaneously. The key rule is that each incentive is assessed against its own threshold, not pooled.

**Which US cities require employers to offer commuter benefits?**
As of 2026, mandates apply in New York City, Washington DC, the San Francisco Bay Area, New Jersey, Chicago, Philadelphia and Seattle. Thresholds vary: New York City and Washington DC cover employers with 20 or more employees, while the Bay Area regional mandate starts at 50. Most require offering pre-tax transit or vanpool benefits, and penalties escalate for non-compliance, so check the ordinance for each jurisdiction where you operate.
