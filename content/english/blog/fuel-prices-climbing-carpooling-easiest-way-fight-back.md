Filling up has rarely felt this painful — no matter which side of the world you're on.

- **In the UK**, average diesel prices have climbed to around **191p per litre** in April 2026, up roughly **39p in six weeks**. A full tank now costs nearly **£100 — about £21 more** than it did in late February.
- **In the US**, the national average crossed **$4 per gallon** for the first time since August 2022, with California hitting **$5.89/gallon** and diesel topping **$6/gallon** in many states. Americans now spend close to **$200 per month on average** just on fuel.
- **In Australia**, petrol jumped from around **A$1.69/L to A$2.19/L** in a matter of weeks, with some regional areas pushing past A$2.40/L and diesel breaking A$3/L in several cities. Even a temporary federal excise cut hasn't fully absorbed the shock.

And this isn't just a geopolitical blip. Even if the current crisis eases, the long-term trajectory is clear: **commuting by car is getting more expensive, and it's not going back to where it was.**

## What this really costs your team

For a typical employee driving 30 miles each way to work, fuel costs have quietly jumped by **£30–£80 / $50–$100 / A$80–A$150** per month since late February. That's up to **$1,000–$1,500 a year**, straight out of their pocket — with no raise attached.

Across the UK, US and Australia, the commute has become **the single fastest-growing line** in many employees' personal budgets.

## Carpooling: the math is hard to ignore

Sharing a ride with **one** coworker cuts fuel costs in half. With **two**, the driver actually comes out ahead.

- A solo driver spending $200/month on fuel drops to **$100/month** when sharing with one coworker
- Across a year, a regular carpooler saves **$1,000 to $2,000** (£800 to £1,600 / A$1,500 to A$3,000)
- In the **US**, employer-sponsored vanpool and commuter benefits can be offered with **pre-tax dollars** under IRS Section 132
- In the **UK**, HMRC **Mileage Allowance Payments** include a 5p-per-mile tax-free passenger supplement when employees carpool for work
- In **Australia**, workplace ride-sharing arrangements can reduce FBT exposure while giving staff real relief from pump prices

## It's not just about the money

Carpooling also:

- Cuts commute-related carbon emissions — a growing requirement for **Scope 3 ESG reporting**
- Reduces parking pressure at the office
- Builds real connections between coworkers who'd otherwise never meet
- Makes hybrid schedules easier to coordinate, not harder

## Why most companies haven't fixed this yet

The idea is simple. The execution is the hard part.

WhatsApp groups die after two weeks. Spreadsheets nobody updates. Awkward "hey, can I chip in for gas?" conversations. For most teams, the friction of organizing carpooling outweighs the savings — so nothing ever happens.
