## Methodology: how this calculator estimates your savings

The calculator takes five inputs — headcount in scope, round-trip commuting distance, working days per year, share of employees currently driving alone, and your carpooling target — and derives three families of result: CO₂ avoided, savings per carpooler, and parking spaces freed.

**The CO₂ calculation.** We first estimate annual single-occupancy mileage: round-trip distance × working days × employees in scope. The share of that mileage shifting to carpooling is then converted into avoided emissions using an average passenger-car emission factor. For France we use the [ADEME emission factor database](https://base-empreinte.ademe.fr/); for the UK and the US we use published EPA factors. Note that two people sharing a ride does not halve emissions in carbon-accounting terms: what counts is the vehicle taken off the road, not the number of occupants in the remaining one.

**The employee savings calculation.** It combines the fuel price and average consumption you enter. The result is a fuel saving, and it is deliberately conservative: it excludes vehicle wear, insurance and parking costs, which in practice make up a significant share of the real cost of a daily commute.

**Parking spaces.** These are derived from vehicles removed at peak times, not from the number of carpoolers — two employees sharing a ride free one space, not two.

**What the calculator does not do.** It does not model ramp-up: results describe a steady state once your carpooling target is reached, not year one. It also ignores the actual geography of your workforce, which is the single biggest limiting factor on any programme. Treat the output as an order of magnitude for budgeting, to be refined with your own data.
